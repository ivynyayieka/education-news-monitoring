Boost Sino-African educational ties
China Daily — 17 Oct 2026

The future of China-Africa relations hinges significantly on the collaborative efforts of our universities. Universities are not merely institutions that hand out degrees; they are epicenters of knowledge, developers of human capital, incubators of innovation and custodians of cultural heritage. Higher education is being reshaped by artificial intelligence, automation, biotechnology, climate change, renewable energy and the fourth industrial revolution, all of which are redefining the skills that societies need.

For Africa, these shifts present both opportunities and challenges. While the continent has the world's youngest population and enormous human potential, its universities still struggle due to limited access, quality issues, financial constraints, inadequate infrastructure and a widening gap between education and the labor market.

The challenge is no longer about producing more graduates, but about cultivating graduates who can generate knowledge, create employment and devise solutions.

Ethiopia Reaches Eritrean Border; M23 Rebrands With Kabila: Africa File, October 15, 2026
Critical Threats — 16 Oct 2026

Ethiopian federal forces consolidated control over all major cities in Tigray and advanced toward the Ethiopia-Eritrea border before taking an operational pause since October 12. Federal forces advanced along several axes to capture the remaining belt of key Tigrayan cities and pushed toward the Eritrean border. The Ethiopian military (Ethiopian National Defense Force, ENDF) and allied Tigray Peace Force (TPF) captured the North Tigray zone capital Adigrat on October 9 and 10 and had advanced 20 miles north to the Ethiopian border town of Zalambessa by October 12. [1] Eritrean forces had controlled the town since the end of the Tigray War in 2022. [2] Ethiopian federal forces on the central axis rapidly advanced 50 miles between October 10 and 11 to capture the Central Tigray zone capital, Adwa. [3] Federal forces further to the west captured the North Western zone capital, Shire, on October 8 and advanced nearly 40 miles east to take the city of Axum by October 12. [4] ENDF forces separately advanced more than 50 miles northwest from Shire toward the Ethiopian border town of Sheraro and reached the town’s outskirts on October 12. [5]

Source: Liam Karr and Michael DeAngelo; Alexander Brown and Ziv Zellars.

Note: ENDF is short for Ethiopian National Defense Force, TPLF is short for Tigray People’s Liberation Front, TDF is short for Tigray Defense Forces.

UNESCO — 14 Oct 2026

Education is the most powerful investment countries can make, yet it is being systematically underfunded. Our projections show that global aid to education will decline by up to 30% between 2023 and 2027, perpetuating a cycle of underinvestment, inequality, and stalled development. Innovative financing mechanisms such as debt-for-education swaps already exist – they only need political will to be scaled up.

Press Corner: Aid to education report, Debt and education series .

New research released today by the UNESCO Global Education Monitoring Report on the occasion of the Transforming Education Summit (TES + 4 ) highlights a continued decline in international aid to education, with a decrease by 8% in 2024 compared with the previous year, while aid to basic education (comprising only pre-school, primary and lower secondary levels) fell by 15%.
