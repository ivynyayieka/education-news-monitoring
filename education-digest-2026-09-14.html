Malawi exam results move online
ITWeb Africa — 9 Sep 2026

Malawi's Ministry of Education and the Malawi National Examinations Board (MANEB) released the 2026 Malawi School Certificate of Education (MSCE) examination results on Monday, with candidates able to access them online.

Previously, printed results had to be physically delivered to districts, creating challenges for remote schools and straining resources.

That changed in 2022, when the board introduced an e-registration system that allowed examination centres to register candidates online.

In 2024, the board introduced e-results portals, allowing candidates and examination centres to access examination results directly, while examination fees can be paid through banks and mobile wallets.

“Examination centres have registration portals where they capture and register candidates, and the same portals are the ones through which they access their examination results in the comfort of their homes,” MANEB public relations manager Angella Kashitigu told ITWeb Africa in an interview.

A child who learns to read with understanding in the early grades is more likely to succeed throughout school, develop confidence and access greater opportunities later in life. A child who struggles to acquire foundational literacy and numeracy skills faces a much steeper path.

Sierra Leone's back-to-school moment must therefore also be a learning moment.

Beyond digital as usual
Unicef — 9 Sep 2026

UNICEF and our strategic partners are articulating a new vision to leverage Artificial Intelligence (AI), educational technology (EdTech) and other innovative approaches through our new Digital Education Strategy. The "Beyond Digital as Usual" strategy focuses on teacher empowerment, foundational learning, skills and competencies, systems strengthening and thought leadership.

It represents a paradigm shift in how we approach technology in education. We are moving beyond fragmented pilots to coordinated, strategic interventions that add real value by breaking down barriers and accelerating learning.

UNICEF’s role is to act as a trusted systems partner, convenor and catalyst, accompanying governments and partners to scale evidence-based solutions responsibly.

The initial phase of the strategy will scale innovative, AI and EdTech-enabled teaching and learning programmes across 18 countries: Benin, Bolivia, Brazil, Chad, Côte d’Ivoire, Egypt, Ghana, Guinea, India, Jordan, Kenya, Kyrgyzstan, Lao PDR, Pakistan, Philippines, Sudan, Tanzania and Ukraine.
