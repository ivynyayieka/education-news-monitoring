Stanford GSB Economists Shape an In-Depth Look at AI’s Global Promise
Stanford Graduate School of Business — 29 Oct 2026

Earlier technological advances such as electricity and the internet took decades to reach the world’s poorest countries. As artificial intelligence spreads faster than anything before it, a new World Bank report finds that developing economies may have a chance to close the gap if they act strategically.

The opportunities created by the rapid diffusion of AI are the focus of the World Development Report 2026, “The Promise of Artificial Intelligence.” The World Bank Group’s flagship annual report presents the first comprehensive assessment of how AI could improve the lives of the 6.8 billion people living in low- and middle-income countries.

The report finds that AI presents an unprecedented chance to accelerate progress at a moment when movement on alleviating extreme poverty has slowed. AI could help developing countries overcome persistent challenges in areas such as agriculture, education, and health care. If developing countries deploy AI strategically and pragmatically, the report concludes, economic and social advances that once would have taken a century might be compressed into a decade.

Light for the World commits US$15 million to inclusive eye health by 2030
Light for the World — 29 Oct 2026

As the first ever Global Summit for Eye Health takes place, Light for the World shares our commitments to strengthen and support comprehensive, inclusive and equitable eye health care in Burkina Faso, Ethiopia, Mozambique and Uganda.

Light for the World will invest US$15 million and reach more than 3.5 million people to advance equitable eye health care in Burkina Faso, Ethiopia, Mozambique and Uganda. We will prioritise people who face the greatest barriers to quality services, including children, women and men with and without disabilities and those in underserved communities.

We commit to delivering people-centred eye care while addressing the gender gap in access to services. This includes gender and disability responsive training, meaningful participation of women, men and people with disabilities in planning, and the collection and use of sex, age and disability disaggregated data to strengthen accountability.

How Community Initiative is bridging education gap for displaced children in Benue
Dubawa — 31 Oct 2026

From struggling to identify the alphabet to reading and writing, children at a community-led school in Benue State are rebuilding their education after displacement. But retaining pupils and sustaining learning remain challenges.

Ten-year-old Amos Terkura’s journey to learning began after he lost his father and fled his community with his mother, Erdoo Terkura, following an attack in November 2021.

For the young boy, reaching an internally displaced persons (IDP) camp at Tse-Yandev, Benue State, meant escaping violence, but it did not immediately restore the childhood opportunities he had lost. Before his displacement, his education had already been disrupted by insecurity and his family’s inability to afford schooling.
